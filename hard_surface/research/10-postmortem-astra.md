## 1. Why the geometry became additive

**The decisive failure was milestone scoping: the plan promised expressive geometry, implementation substituted primitives, and subsequent work optimized that restricted implementation.** Additive-only was not an established research conclusion. [PLAN.md:55–60](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/PLAN.md:55), [M2-BRIEF.md:18–38](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/M2-BRIEF.md:18)

The decision chain, ranked by impact:

1. **Implementation removed capabilities the architecture named.** The authoritative plan includes **“lathe, extrudeProfile(+holes)”**. M2 instead specifies five additional primitives and permits an arch approximation: **“`// MVP-STUB` if approximated by a box.”** The compiler records the resulting decision explicitly: **“A true arch needs an XY-plane extruder … which is more prim vocabulary than M2 is allowed to add.”** That is the clearest decision point. [PLAN.md:55–60](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/PLAN.md:55), [M2-BRIEF.md:18–20](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/M2-BRIEF.md:18), [grammar.js:359–368](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/compile/grammar.js:359)

2. **Research turned an architectural preference into an unsupported guarantee.** Its ranking gives the constrained DSL 5/5 across every dimension and says the owner’s failure modes become “structurally impossible.” The plan repeats that claim. These are architectural judgments; a tenement comparison establishing superiority over CSG/extrusion is **UNVERIFIED**. The source-repo research explicitly leaves **CSG versus extrusion-with-holes** unresolved. [03-landscape.md:183–212](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/03-landscape.md:183), [PLAN.md:18–19](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/PLAN.md:18), [01-source-repos.md:234–238](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/01-source-repos.md:234)

3. **The compiler implements assembly, not solid editing.** It builds each primitive independently, paints and transforms it, then merges the geometry buffers. Windows are additional boxes penetrating the host, with their front surfaces standing proud; they never remove wall geometry. `socket` means penetration, not subtraction. [build.js:164–181](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/compile/build.js:164), [grammar.js:324–337](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/compile/grammar.js:324), [SPEC.md:118–120](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/dsl/SPEC.md:118)

4. **Validator limitations started dictating the appearance.** The original railing acquired an opaque web because the attachment solver could not position its top rail as intended. The awning acquired an overlapping valance to satisfy contact measurement. Those were solver accommodations, not conclusions about the target’s style. [railing.json:5](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/parts/lib/railing.json:5), [awning.json:5](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/parts/lib/awning.json:5)

One correction to the premise: polygon construction exists **inside** the compiler—`loft`, a closed staircase-profile extrusion, chamfered boxes, and a ring with a real bore. What is missing is **author-accessible profiles, mesh operations, and Boolean operations**. [prims.js:128–166](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/prims.js:128), [prims.js:175–260](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/prims.js:175), [prims.js:297–312](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/prims.js:297)

## 2. Was additive-only the right choice?

**For initial massing, yes. As the representation for the finished look, no.** The brief prioritizes correctness, then quality, then iteration in seconds; it explicitly names arched entrances, parapets, balconies and fire escapes. Shipping their placeholders as the refinement baseline under-served that brief. [BRIEF.md:9–15](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/BRIEF.md:9), [BRIEF.md:33–37](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/BRIEF.md:33), [README.md:75–90](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/README.md:75)

The quality limits, in impact order:

- **Composition and major forms were still wrong before topology mattered.** The target exposes the mint corner; the result exposes the blank tan side. The camera uses positive azimuth and a positive-X viewing offset, while the authored mint side windows occupy `−X`. Roof parapets are whole-footprint solids instead of perimeter walls. These are camera/spec decisions that additive geometry could already improve. [camera.json:2–3](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/refs/tenement_block.camera.json:2), [app.js:224–229](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/app.js:224), [spec.json:129–191](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/subjects/tenement_block_llm/spec.json:129), [spec.json:1511–1514](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/subjects/tenement_block_llm/spec.json:1511); visual evidence: [target](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/refs/tenement-iso.png), [result](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/out/m4/final-target.png).

- **Architectural depth became surface decoration.** Window and door “insets” leave the host intact; the arched doorway remains a rectangular panel plus wedge. The compiler cannot express the requested operation of cutting an arched opening or recessed reveal into an existing building mass. [grammar.js:324–337](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/compile/grammar.js:324), [grammar.js:359–375](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/compile/grammar.js:359)

- **Distinctive profiles were unavailable to the author.** Arbitrary extruded outlines, profiles with holes, selectable-edge bevels, and user-defined loft sections have no DSL representation. Existing `bevelBox`/`plate` bevels do exist; claiming “no bevels” would be incorrect. A faceted arch or rectangular roof recess can be approximated by assembling surrounding pieces, but the DSL provides no direct profile/cut operation. [SPEC.md:32–59](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/dsl/SPEC.md:32), [prims.js:59](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/prims.js:59), [prims.js:297](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/prims.js:297)

- **Open ironwork was initially blocked by attachment algebra, not by additive construction.** M6’s `alignU` enabled open railings; r8 then replaced filled railings and the solid stair with bars, stringers and individual treads. That directly demonstrates that missing CSG explains only part of the visual gap. [SPEC.md:136–144](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/dsl/SPEC.md:136), [r8/log.md:17–23](D:/Git/checkout/tools/BlindLoop/runs/m4-tenement-r8/log.md:17)

**Correctness was overstated.** Contact calculations substitute bounding boxes for actual surfaces; V3 explicitly says its real Boolean cross-check is deferred. Even the research warned against bounding-box-only structural validation. [obb.js:7–11](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/validate/obb.js:7), [v3.js:7–16](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/validate/v3.js:7), [02-correctness.md:214–218](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/02-correctness.md:214)

**Speed was measured at the wrong level:** M1 compile/validation took 28 ms, while the documented improvement round took approximately ten minutes. A Boolean/extrusion runtime comparison is **UNVERIFIED**. The recorded bottleneck was orchestration and model calls. [README.md:85–90](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/README.md:85), [WORKFLOW.md:116–123](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/WORKFLOW.md:116)

Missing profiles therefore became parameter-search problems inside an insufficient vocabulary. ## 6. Concrete v2 — proposed, not implemented

**Build a hybrid procedural modeler: profile extrusion for architecture, CSG for cuts, ordinary meshes for assembled detail.** This implements the expressive geometry already named in the plan. [PLAN.md:55–60](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/PLAN.md:55)

- **Representation:** one `asset.ts` exporting named parts, geometry, palette zones and attachment frames. Permit polygon profiles with holes, extrusion, lofts, explicit transforms and controlled mesh operations. Use `manifold-3d` for Boolean differences/unions where solid editing is needed. Its maintained JS/WASM package supports mesh Booleans and vertex properties; project-specific speed and colour preservation remain **UNVERIFIED** until tested. [Manifold documentation](https://github.com/elalish/manifold), [01-source-repos.md:170–173](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/research/01-source-repos.md:170)
- **Construction:** extruded wall profiles or shell-minus-cutters for openings; shaped profiles for cornices, bulkheads and awnings; bars and treads for ironwork. Merge drawable geometry after construction. Do not require the bench, crates and buildings to become one physical solid merely to produce one render mesh. The current V3 conflates those requirements. [v3.js:55–57](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/validate/v3.js:55)
- **Validators:** retain finite geometry, units, bounds, winding, thickness, budgets, palette/export and determinism checks. Validate actual generated surfaces for contact and penetration; check support per intended assembly. Keep semantic counts and orientation as explicit assertions. Bounding boxes remain broad-phase approximations, not final proof. [PLAN.md:102–114](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/PLAN.md:102), [obb.js:7–11](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/validate/obb.js:7)
- **Workflow:** target-aware authoring with a warm preview; one independent global review at major shape milestones. BlindLoop begins with uncertain refinements. Every candidate and accepted merge validates the exact asset revision. [WORKFLOW.md:80–89](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/WORKFLOW.md:80)
- **Delete from the active design:** the eight-primitive authoring ceiling, mandatory single-parent placement for every detail, automatic opening deletion, full-suite-only candidate gating, and three-by-three voting for obvious construction corrections. Retain the run history as evidence. These changes directly address the documented restrictions and incidents. [SPEC.md:30–59](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/dsl/SPEC.md:30), [grammar.js:450–470](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/src/compile/grammar.js:450), [package.json:11](D:/Git/checkout/tools/LLMModeling/package.json:11)
