# Blend Fourier tubes into one skeletal field

The best 3D counterpart of the 2D outline experiment is not a single global spherical-harmonic series. It is the representation the in-house prototype already uses, with one change. Keep a **branching skeleton of tubes** (a cosine-series centre line plus a Fourier cross-section whose coefficients vary along the tube, N along by M around). Then stop meshing each tube on its own: **evaluate every tube as an approximate signed distance, fuse them with a smooth-min, and mesh the fused field once**. The literature supports the parts of this design. Skeleton-plus-radius is the established compact recipe for creatures: Spore used a skeleton-driven metaball skin, and B-Mesh builds a base mesh from a skeleton with "key balls" ([Hecker](https://www.chrishecker.com/My_Liner_Notes_for_Spore); [B-Mesh](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v29i7pp2169-2177)). Smooth implicit blending is the standard way to remove seams where parts overlap ([Calgary BlobTree](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~blob/webpapers/05/aesthetics.pdf)). LLMs build better geometry from structured, code-like construction trees than from raw coordinates ([arXiv 2510.14980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14980)). No source tests the whole combination. Two things here are in-house inference, the result of joining those threads with our own observations: the specific "Fourier-tube SDF" primitive, and the claim that it best fits LLM-from-memory authoring. Our own experiments supply the evidence that it works at all. Blind Opus and Fable agents each built a recognisable 3D wolf in `harness/fourier3d.py` with roughly 2,000-5,000 coefficients, and the main defect was hard seams where tubes overlap, which is exactly what smooth blending addresses. The recommended pipeline has four steps. The LLM writes a small symmetric JSON skeleton with low-order Fourier tubes. A compiler turns it into a blended field, meshes it and skins it from the tubes that generated each vertex. A loop of numeric checks and multi-view renders sends corrections back to the LLM. The result exports as an anyCreature chain spec.

## Global harmonics fail because creatures are branching tubes

The direct 3D analogue of elliptic Fourier descriptors is SPHARM. It maps a genus-0 surface to the sphere and expands x, y and z in spherical harmonics, and truncating the degree gives coarser levels of detail ([Brechbuhler et al., CVIU 1995](https://www.datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/1077-3142/volumes-and-issues/2/paper-detail/70695); [PMC3062073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3062073)). It is the elegant answer on paper, and the wrong one for this task. It requires spherical topology ([SPHARM-PDM](https://insight-journal.org/browse/publication/101)). It **rings near rapidly changing regions** and needs weighting to suppress that ([Chung](https://pages.stat.wisc.edu/~mchung/papers/SSP/SSP2007.asymmetry.poster.pdf)). Even a smooth organ like the hippocampus was truncated at **degree L=20** ([arXiv 1707.05961](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05961)). By standard counting that is 3(L+1)^2 = 1,323 coefficients, and it describes a blob, not a wolf with thin legs, ears and a tail.

The inference that matters most is about authorability. A model can reason "harmonic 3 makes the outline lobed" in 2D, but high-degree SH coefficients mix shape with the parametrization and carry no intuitive meaning, so an LLM cannot write them from memory. The other bases are ruled out on different grounds. Manifold harmonics are computed from an existing mesh ([Vallet & Levy](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v27i2pp251-260)). Kazhdan descriptors and Shape-DNA are retrieval fingerprints that cannot be inverted back to a shape ([Kazhdan 2003](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~funk/sgp03.pdf); [Reuter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5015)). Radial SH, r(theta, phi), is authorable but works only for star-shaped objects.

That leaves the generalized cylinder, a cross-section swept along an axis ([arXiv 1911.00571](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00571)). A paper on modelling generalized cylinders with Fourier-morphed cross-sections exists, but only its title was verified ([Aguado et al.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/253717)). A tube with a Fourier cross-section is the honest 3D generalization of the 2D trick. Along each limb it is still "truncate to K harmonics and rebuild", and every coefficient keeps its meaning: harmonic 0 is radius, harmonic 1 is an offset, harmonic 2 is ellipticity. SMAL is useful as a sanity bound. It spans cats, dogs, horses, cows and hippos with a **41-dimensional shape space** on a 35-joint skeleton ([AniMer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00837)). That suggests quadruped proportions are low-dimensional. Our 2,000-5,000 coefficients are probably mostly detail headroom, and a recognisable wolf should need only a few hundred numbers. That estimate is in-house inference, not a measured result.

## A smooth-min field turns tube seams into fillets

The math is the main recommendation. Each tube i has a centre line c_i(t), t in [0, 1], written as a short cosine series (keep the current form). Its cross-section is a polar radius r_i(t, theta) = a0(t) + sum_m [a_m(t) cos(m theta) + b_m(t) sin(m theta)], with each coefficient a cosine series in t. The change is how a tube is evaluated. Instead of only sweeping it into a mesh, define an approximate distance d_i(p): find the closest t* on c_i, express p in that frame as (rho, theta), and take d_i = rho - r_i(t*, theta). The body is then **D(p) = smin_k(d_1, ..., d_n)**, with a blend radius k per joint (for example, a large k at the shoulder, a small k at the toes).

Implicit modelling has used this idea for decades. Union is min, and soft blending of skeletal primitives gives smooth, bulge-free junctions ([varun.ca](https://varun.ca/ray-march-sdf/); [Bloomenthal](https://unchainedgeometry.com/JBloom/papers.html)). Calgary's work argues that **summing distance-based skeletal primitives avoids the cost of convolution surfaces** and still blends cleanly ([Galbraith et al.](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~blob/pdf/2004_cgi_tree_modeling.pdf)). Spore shipped the same structure in a consumer game: one implicit skin of polynomial metaballs, f = s[(p-c)^2/R^2 - 1]^4, generated from the creature's spine and limbs. Hecker chose it because **topological robustness mattered more than local control** ([Hecker](https://www.chrishecker.com/My_Liner_Notes_for_Spore)).

The exact smin formula (the polynomial "quadratic" form is the usual cheap, local choice) comes from background knowledge. It was not verified here, because iquilezles.org could not be fetched. Two practical cautions are in-house inference. First, keep the radius derivatives modest, so that |dr/dt| and |dr/dtheta|/r stay below about 1. Otherwise d_i stops being a usable distance bound and the blends misbehave. Second, a larger k gives softer fillets but swallows detail, so ears and toes need a small k.

Mesh the fused field once with marching cubes, the standard route for implicits ([Wikipedia: Metaballs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaballs)). Then decimate it to budget, or follow B-Mesh's lead and use the skeleton to drive a quad-dominant base mesh whose edge flow follows the bones ([B-Mesh](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v29i7pp2169-2177)). For the RTX 2070 Mobile / GTX 1050 target, bake to a mesh offline and never raymarch at runtime. Full raymarching of a character, as in Selfie Girl, is heavy on weak GPUs ([MetaFilter](https://metafilter.com/194834/Paint-the-universe-with-geometry-smoothstep-and-noise)). Grid resolution (64^3-128^3 before decimation) is an unbenchmarked starting point.

## Have the LLM write a symmetric, named skeleton with low-order Fourier terms

The authoring format should play to what LLMs do well and fence off what they do badly. Construction trees beat coordinate dumps ([arXiv 2510.14980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14980)). Spatial construction fails more often than planning does. The dominant errors are orientation, wrong parent attachment and collisions, with **collisions making up 69.1% of craft-assembly failures** ([Prompt2Craft](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.04568)). Models also violate value ranges they were told about ([ShapeCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11680)). Our own 2D finding fits this pattern: both Opus and Fable drew two ears in a true side view, which is an orthographic projection error.

The format therefore should be a JSON tree of **named parts** (spine, neck, head, muzzle, ear_L, foreleg_L and so on). Each part has a parent, an attach parameter t on that parent, and a centre line given as a few control points or cosine terms in body-relative units, with the body length set to 1. Each part also has an **explicit low-order cross-section**. Write it as a handful of stations along the tube, each with a radius, an ellipse aspect ratio and orientation, and at most 2-4 extra harmonics, rather than raw N by M coefficient arrays. Symmetry is declared, not drawn. The LLM writes only the left side and sets `mirror: true`, which removes the "two ears in side view" class of error at the source. A compiler, not the LLM, should fit the stations to the cosine-in-t series and clamp ranges. Under this design a wolf lands in the **hundreds of numbers rather than thousands**, which is again an inference and needs measuring. The 2D experiment can carry over directly as a benchmark. Ask the LLM to produce stations at high order, fit them, truncate to K harmonics along and around, rebuild, and plot recognisability against K.

## Close the loop with numbers first, then renders

Render-and-revise helps, but vision alone is not enough. In CADSmith, nested loops of execution checks, geometric measurements and a VLM judge raised median IoU from **0.81 to 0.96** and cut mean Chamfer distance from **28.37 to 0.74**. The authors argue that visual-only feedback misses dimensional errors ([arXiv 2603.26512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26512)). In CADCodeVerify, VLM self-questioning gained 7.3% on point-cloud distance ([ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/81a934cd364e18ea6fdeaf57a93c17d4-Paper-Conference.pdf)). GPT-4 fixed obvious parametric mistakes but **missed common-sense visual errors** ([3D-PreMise](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.06437)). LL3M and the agentic pelican experiments use render critique as the core of their loop ([LL3M](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08228); [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/11/agentic-pelican-on-a-bicycle/)).

The loop should therefore start with automatic checks. These cover proportion ratios (leg length to body, head to body), ground contact of all four feet, left/right symmetry residuals, self-intersection of unrelated parts before blending, tube radius-derivative bounds, and silhouette area per view. Only then should it show the model orthographic **front, side, top and three-quarter** renders and ask targeted questions ("how many ears are visible from the side?"). The value of multiple views over a single view is untested in the sources and is our inference. It is directly motivated by the in-house orthographic errors.

For evaluation, no published blind-recognition protocol for LLM-made shapes was found. The pelican test is judged by eye ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/11/agentic-pelican-on-a-bicycle/)). The protocol has to be ours: a fresh VLM or human names the animal from renders, with no prompt context, and is scored against harmonic budget K. Keep the per-model blind builds (Opus wolf, Fable wolf) as regression cases.

## Skin weights come free from the generating tube

The path into the engine is short because the representation is already a skeleton. Spore derived bone weights from **which body part generated each metaball** ([Hecker](https://www.chrishecker.com/My_Liner_Notes_for_Spore)). The same works here: at each mesh vertex, weight bone j by its tube's share of the smin blend, with a smoothstep falloff along t between joints, and keep the top four influences.

The fallback is proximity-plus-diffusion skinning. Pinocchio rigged 13 of 16 test characters automatically, but it produces LBS artifacts ([Baran & Popovic](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ibaran/papers/2007-SIGGRAPH-Pinocchio.pdf)). Because anyCreature already builds skinned, rigged GLBs from a JSON spec of chains with per-joint cross-section profiles, there are two routes. The cheapest is to have the compiler emit that spec directly, sampling each tube's Fourier cross-section at the joints into the existing profile format. That gets rigged output immediately, at the cost of the engine's own (unblended) junction handling. The better route is to add a blended-field mesher as an engine option, gated as a default-off flag, and assign weights by blend share. Spore's warning applies to both: **long spine segments and fat torsos shear** under skinning ([Hecker](https://www.chrishecker.com/My_Liner_Notes_for_Spore)), so keep spine joints dense where the torso is thick.

## Conclusion

The 2D lesson carries over, but the unit of truncation changes. In 2D the whole silhouette was one closed curve, so one Fourier series fitted it. In 3D a creature is a tree, so the compact math has to be **many tiny Fourier series attached to a skeleton**, joined by a blend operator rather than by geometry. Seams were never a Fourier problem. They come from treating overlapping tubes as separate surfaces, and a single smin field solves them at the cost of one more parameter per joint.

The LLM-facing lesson is that the from-memory failures we saw (an extra ear, likely wrong limb attachments in 3D) are structural. Prompting will not remove them; the format must, through declared symmetry, named parents and compiler-enforced ranges, with geometric checks catching what renders miss. The first experiments to run are three. First, re-score the existing Opus and Fable wolves after smin blending. Second, run the harmonic-budget sweep to find the real K for "recognisable wolf". Third, compare raw N by M coefficients against the station format on blind recognition. Together they would replace this report's main inferences with in-house numbers.
